## Operating Order

Anthony's sequence is the runner contract:

1. Map long-tail keywords and general search roots.
   - Goal: build a huge network of phrases that can establish the DigitalHut brand before chasing only short competitive keywords.
   - Include original roots: 3D experience, 3D images, 3D environments, 3D presentation, real world visualization, AI guided 3D presentation, 3D observatory dapp, GLB renderer, home observatory, family observatory, premium observatory.

2. Map regional, foreign, and unusual markets that can contain large overlooked audiences.
   - United States lanes: West Coast, East Coast, Midwest/Mid-Coast, South, California tourism, New York/New Jersey urban search, Texas real estate, Florida travel, Midwest family/education, university/research markets, gaming communities, local business markets, and non-obvious visual-search lanes.
   - International lanes: China, Europe, Middle East, Africa, Latin America, South Asia, Caribbean/island tourism, family travel, research/education, local real estate, gaming communities, and non-obvious visual-search lanes.
   - Purpose: avoid only competing in one saturated startup/search lane. DigitalHut should build search roots across U.S. regions and global markets.

3. Translate, transcribe, and localize the information into keyword lanes those markets can actually hit.
   - Do not only write English keywords.
   - For U.S. markets, create regional/local phrases such as Los Angeles 3D observatory experience, New York 3D city visualization, Texas real estate 3D walkthrough, Florida family travel 3D experience, Midwest school 3D learning environment.
   - For international markets, create translated variants, transliterated variants when useful, and plain-English versions for the blog.
   - Each market lane should include: region, language/local angle, search phrase, DigitalHut category, thumbnail idea, GLB/render idea, blog angle, and expected user action.

4. Update the blog from the master SEO list.
   - Each blog should come from the current master list, not random inspiration.
   - Each blog needs: primary long-tail keyword, supporting keyword cluster, thumbnail target, linked 3D render or Play Preview, internal backlink, and pixel tracking goal.
   - The blog should make the page feel like DigitalHut: observatory, 3D renderer, AI-guided presentation, real-world visualization, and category movement.

5. Record the new SEO feel for the page.
   - After each blog/content update, write a short report describing what the page now feels like from an SEO/product perspective.
   - Report what audience the page appears to be speaking to, what keywords are now stronger, what visual/render path is expected, and what metric should prove it.

6. Map the new master SEO list.
   - Merge the new blog, market, translation, thumbnail, and render evidence back into the master list.
   - Promote winning phrases.
   - Mark weak phrases as pending, untested, or retired.

7. Build the regional-market and master-keyword system inside FireCuda.
   - FireCuda folders should preserve original captures, optimized GLBs, thumbnails, SEO evidence, blog history, backlink notes, failed renders, winning assets, U.S. region maps, global market maps, translation/localization notes, and reports.
   - FireCuda is the evidence room for the next SEO/content cycle.

8. Report the information back to Anthony while the work is happening.
   - Reporting is not a separate later feature. It happens simultaneously.
   - Each cycle report should say: what was mapped, what changed, what was tested, what looks stronger, what looks weak, what should be done next.

9. Rinse and repeat.
   - Tighten keywords from real behavior.
   - Improve thumbnails.
   - Improve GLB preview path.
   - Publish stronger blogs.
   - Archive evidence.
   - Repeat with better assets and better regional targeting.

## SEO Framework Capacity Model

The SEO framework is too deep for a shallow "one prompt, one blog" runner. It requires staged processing:

1. Intake layer:
   - Collect DigitalHut pixel activity, blog reads, thumbnail clicks, GLB plays, search terms, wallet/tier/node interest, and failed render paths.
   - Pull current FireCuda evidence: original captures, optimized GLBs, thumbnails, blog drafts, backlink notes, and winning assets.

2. Research layer:
   - Compare long-tail search phrases against competitor surfaces such as 3D model viewers, GLB marketplaces, Google Images behavior, presentation websites, and observatory/science visualization content.
   - Separate broad keywords from realistic entry phrases.

3. Mapping layer:
   - Convert research into market lanes: U.S. West Coast, U.S. East Coast, U.S. Midwest/Mid-Coast, U.S. South, China, Europe, Middle East, Africa, Latin America, Caribbean tourism, research/education, real estate, gamer, planetary, and family observatory.
   - Attach each lane to thumbnail naming, blog title, internal backlink, category, GLB environment, and expected user action.

4. Build layer:
   - Update DigitalHut pages, metadata, thumbnails, blog cards, category feeds, insight map metrics, and renderer paths.
   - Keep the work deployable. Do not add heavy assets to Git unless intentionally approved.

5. Verification layer:
   - Run build checks.
   - Confirm `/blog`, `/insights`, and renderer routes still load.
   - Watch the insight map for thumbnail-to-render clicks and GLB plays.

6. Repeat layer:
   - Use the next runner cycle to adjust keywords and assets from evidence.
   - Preserve the result in FireCuda and the repo docs.

This is the reason higher Codex operating capacity matters: it gives the manual runner enough context and time to hold the entire loop at once instead of losing the sequence.
